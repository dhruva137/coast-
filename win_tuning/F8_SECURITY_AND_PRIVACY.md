# F8 — Security & Privacy
*"Data protection & compliance with privacy regulations"* · Round 1

---

## A. Why this is our easiest 10 — and our easiest 0

Most teams treat privacy as a slide that says "we take privacy seriously." That
scores 5.

We have something rare: **a genuinely checkable privacy property.** The app is
fully on-device. No user data leaves the phone. A judge can verify this from the
manifest and the source in under a minute.

But the same property that makes it a 10 makes it a 0 if we overstate it by even
one word. **We previously had exactly that failure** — a draft claimed "no
INTERNET permission" when the manifest declares `INTERNET` for OSM basemap tiles.
That was caught and corrected. It must never come back.

**The rule: state the property precisely, and let the precision be the proof.**

## B. The claim, worded exactly

> **No user data leaves the device.** Position, sensor data, and trajectories are
> computed and stored entirely on the phone. The app declares `INTERNET` for one
> purpose only — fetching public OpenStreetMap/Carto basemap tiles. **With the
> basemap turned off, network traffic is zero.** The optional phone-tracker is
> opt-in, off by default, and posts only to an address on your own LAN that you
> type in yourself.

Every clause is verifiable. That is the point.

**Never say:** "no INTERNET permission" · "zero network" without the basemap-off
qualifier · "GDPR compliant" (we have not done a compliance assessment) ·
"encrypted" (say what is encrypted, or say nothing) · "anonymous" (there is no
data to anonymise — say that instead).

## C. What the audit already confirmed

An independent read-only audit found the Android side genuinely clean:

- **No analytics, no telemetry, no third-party SDK.**
- **No secrets, tokens, or API keys** anywhere in `web/`, `android/app/src/main/java/`, or `lab/`.
- **No hardcoded IP addresses.** The only match is placeholder hint text in
  `SettingsScreen.kt:290`; `Prefs.kt:86` defaults `trackerLanIp` to `""` and
  `trackerOptIn` to `false`.
- **No logging** (`Log.d`/`println`) in Android main.
- The `LanUploader` exists **only** under `src/tracker/` — the `standard` flavour
  has no uploader at all.

This is a strong position. The work is to make it *visible*.

## D. The three artifacts

### 8.1 — The privacy proof (Phase 4, Subagent 4.1)  ← the 10/10 artifact

Do not assert privacy. **Generate it from the source.**

`tools/privacy_report.py` → `docs/PRIVACY_REPORT.md`, containing:
- Every permission in every manifest, with file:line and a one-sentence
  justification for each.
- **Every outbound network call site in the codebase**, found by scanning, with
  file:line and purpose. If the list has one entry (tiles), that is the whole
  argument, proven mechanically.
- Confirmation that the `standard` flavour contains no uploader.
- The precise `INTERNET` statement from §B.

Regenerate it inside `verify_all` so it cannot drift from the code.

**Why this scores 10:** every other team will *say* they are private. We hand the
judge a document that was generated by scanning our own source, and invite them
to check it. It is the same move as the claim linter, applied to privacy.

### 8.2 — Airplane-mode demo (Phase 4, Subagent 4.2)

The whole Round-2 demo runs in airplane mode from the bundled MBTiles. This is
simultaneously:
- the **strongest possible privacy demonstration** — the judge watches it work
  with the radio off, and no argument is needed;
- **insurance** against dead venue wifi.

**Turn on airplane mode in front of the judge.** It takes three seconds and it
proves more than the slide does.

### 8.3 — Architectural privacy, stated as a design choice

Three decisions that were made for other reasons and happen to be privacy wins.
Say them as deliberate:

| Decision | Privacy consequence |
|---|---|
| OpenStreetMap, not Google Maps | No third party learns where our users go. A proprietary maps SDK is a telemetry channel by construction. |
| On-device inference (ONNX) | Sensor data never leaves the phone; there is no server to breach. |
| No account required | We hold no identity, so there is nothing to leak, subpoena, or sell. |
| Opt-in LAN-only tracker | Even the optional feature never reaches the internet. |

**The strongest formulation:** *"The most reliable way to protect user data is to
never collect it. We have no server, no account, and no analytics — so there is no
database to breach."* Data minimisation is the actual principle behind most
privacy regulation, and stating it that way lands with a legally-literate judge
without us claiming a compliance certification we do not have.

## E. Security (distinct from privacy — don't conflate them)

- **Attack surface is near-zero by construction:** no inbound network listener in
  the `standard` flavour, no user accounts, no credentials stored.
- **Known item to disclose:** `web/vite.config.ts` binds `host: "::"` — a
  LAN-exposed dev server with a `POST /api/train` that spawns Python. That is
  fine on a demo laptop and should be understood before running it on venue wifi.
  Prefer binding to `localhost` unless the phone genuinely needs to reach it.
  **Fix this before the event, and if asked, say what it was and that we changed it.**
- **Spoofing resistance is a real security angle worth one line:** a system that
  needs no satellite signal cannot be spoofed by a fake satellite signal. When
  GNSS is being actively spoofed, an inertial+map solution is not fooled by the
  attack at all. That is a genuine security property of the architecture, and it
  connects directly to the jamming evidence in `RESEARCH_GLOBAL_CONTEXT.md`.

## H. The questions that decide this criterion

> *"You have the INTERNET permission. So data does leave the phone."*

The answer must be immediate, specific, and unembarrassed — this is the trap
question and hesitating here loses the criterion:

> "Good catch — yes, we declare it, and it's documented in the manifest with a
> comment explaining why. It's used for exactly one thing: fetching public
> OpenStreetMap basemap tiles. It never carries user data. And you can verify
> that two ways: the generated privacy report lists every network call site in
> the codebase, and right now the phone is in airplane mode and the demo is still
> running off the bundled offline map."

> *"Is it GDPR compliant?"*

> "We haven't done a formal compliance assessment, so I won't claim certification.
> What I can tell you is the architecture: we don't collect personal data at all —
> no account, no identifiers, no server. Data minimisation is the core principle
> those regulations are built on, and we satisfy it by having nothing to minimise."
