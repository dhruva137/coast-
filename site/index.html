Why this architecture

A phone on a dashboard sees two superimposed signals: vehicle motion (low band) and road / engine vibration (high band). Rather than filter one away, COAST-VNet-1 splits the band and treats the two as different information, then fuses them into a single forward-speed estimate. 

IMU (20×6) → FrequencySplit
                 ↓
    low  band → Conv1d(6→32) → GRU(32→80, 2 layers)   vehicle motion
                 ↓
    high band → Conv1d(6→48) → GroupNorm → Conv×2    surface / vibration
                                             ↓
                     concat → Linear(135→96) → Linear(96→6)

Measured performance

Protocol: leave-file-out over 23 IO-VNBD drives — train on 22, score the held-out drive only, 12 epochs per fold. Labels are CAN-bus indicated vehicle speed at 10 Hz. Full per-fold results are in the committed summary.

What this release delivers

  • 2.02× lower median position error vs naive DR — measured over 43 GNSS outages with the map-in-loop particle filter.
  • ~8% lower closed-loop distance error vs the strongest naive baseline — wins 9 / 23 leave-file-out folds on drives the model has never seen.
  • Compass drift down from 16.87% to 7.22% — onset-calibrated fusion that runs on the phone during an outage.
  • 98× throughput headroom on the edge engine — worst-case 19,682 Hz against a 200 Hz requirement.
  • 392 KB ONNX, 96,086 parameters, CPU-only — ships inside the APK, runs offline, no cloud dependency.
